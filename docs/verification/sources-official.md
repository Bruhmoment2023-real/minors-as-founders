# sources-official.md - Indian provisions re-read on official text

Research record only. Not legal advice. Work done 13-14 October 2026. Nothing here is manuscript prose.

## How to read the labels
- **read on official source via Internet Archive** = the official PDF or page (India Code, mca.gov.in) as captured by the Wayback Machine ("id_" raw capture). Live indiacode.nic.in returned 403 and mca.gov.in blocked automated readers, so every Indian statute below is from an archive copy of the official file. Capture date is in the "Copy" column. Re-open the live official page before quoting; the "accessed" date in the paper must be Sean's own.
- **read on secondary source** = a reproduction or report that is not the official text.
- **not read** = looked for, not obtained.
- Saved copies are in `followup\raw\` (file names in the "Copy" column). Fetch log: `followup\raw\_fetch-log.txt`.

## Answer first: the 18-year minimum for a Director Identification Number

**It exists, but only in an MCA administrative instruction, not in the Act or in the Rules text I could read.**

- Official text, MCA "Instruction Kit for eForm DIR-3 (Application for allotment of Director Identification Number)", field 7 "Date of Birth": "Enter date in DD/MM/YYYY format even if its proof contains date in any other format. Proof is mandatory and should be valid, clearly visible and duly attested. **Person should be minimum 18 years of age while applying for this application.**"
  - Label: read on official source via Internet Archive. Two captures, same sentence in both: 3 May 2014 (`mca_wb_DIR3_help_2014.pdf`, p. 5 of 16) and 6 June 2023 (`mca_wb_DIR3_help_20230606.pdf`, extracted to `mca_wb_DIR3_help_20230606_pymupdf.txt`; the archive copy is cut off at 1,048,576 bytes but the page with this sentence is intact).
  - The kit says it implements "Section 153 of the Companies Act" and "Rule 9(1) of the Companies (Appointment and Qualification Of Directors) Rules, 2014" and reproduces both. It is an instruction on how to fill the form; its legal force as a condition of eligibility is a question for a lawyer (see lawyer-check.md, Q2).
  - The 2023 kit adds a note that anyone intending to become a director "in a new Company" applies for a DIN "only through SPICe eform". The age rule for the SPICe route was **not read** (SPICe+/INC-32 instructions not obtained).
- Where the age is **not** stated (all read on official source via Internet Archive):
  - Companies Act 2013 s. 153, 154, 155 (DIN): no age.
  - s. 152(3): "No person shall be appointed as a director of a company unless he has been allotted the Director Identification Number under section 154 or any other number as may be prescribed under section 153." This is the link that turns the DIN instruction into a practical bar.
  - s. 164(1)(a)-(i): the list of disqualifications has no age ground.
  - s. 149(1): "a Board of Directors consisting of individuals as directors" - no age.
  - Rule 9 (application for DIN) as it appears in the MCA's Chapter 11 rules text (March 2014 capture, a pre-notification copy headed "TO BE PUBLISHED IN THE GAZETTE", so **not the notified text**): no age. The notified Rules were not obtained. The MCA's 2018 amendment notification (G.S.R. 431(E), 7 May 2018; `mca_wb_ApptDirRules_2018amend.pdf`) lists the principal Rules as G.S.R. 259(E) of 31 [month not legible in the extracted text], 2014, then amendments G.S.R. 671(E) (2014), 42(E) (2015), 839(E) (2017) and 51(E) (2018). That amendment concerns the independent-directors data bank (rule 5) and has no bearing on age.
  - MCA FAQ on the DIN process (5 Nov 2016 capture): "Every individual, intending to be appointed as a director of the company, can file an application for allotment of DIN." No age.
  - LLP Rules 2009 as notified (Dec 2012 capture), r. 10 and Form 7 (DPIN): date-of-birth proof required, no age.
- Companion fact: the only statutory age floor for a director is **21**, for a managing director, whole-time director or manager (s. 196(3)(a), below).
- Verdict to carry forward: "No statutory minimum age for an ordinary director was found; an 18-year floor appears in the MCA's own DIR-3 instruction kit (2014 and 2023 captures)." Practitioner notes (IndiaFilings, CA B K Goyal, LegalWiz) cited no provision; the kit is probably their source. That is an inference, not something they say.

## 1. Indian Contract Act 1872
Copy: `ic_wb_contract_20251012.pdf` (India Code PDF, capture 12 Oct 2025). Label: read on official source via Internet Archive.

- **s. 10**: "All agreements are contracts if they are made by the free consent of parties competent to contract, for a lawful consideration and with a lawful object, and are not hereby expressly declared to be void."
- **s. 11**: "Every person is competent to contract who is of the age of majority according to the law to which he is subject, and who is of sound mind, and is not disqualified from contracting by any law to which he is subject." (Footnote 3 on the PDF: "See the Indian Majority Act, 1875".)
- **s. 65**: "When an agreement is discovered to be void, or when a contract becomes void, any person who has received any advantage under such agreement or contract is bound to restore it, or to make compensation for it ..." (text continues; read only the opening). Mohori Bibee held s. 65 inapplicable to a minor's agreement because "there never was, and never could have been, any contract" (see cases.md).
- **s. 68**: "If a person, incapable of entering into a contract, or any one whom he is legally bound to support, is supplied by another person with necessaries suited to his condition in life, the person who has furnished such supplies is entitled to be reimbursed from the property of such incapable person."
- **s. 183**: "Any person who is of the age of majority according to the law to which he is subject, and who is of sound mind, may employ an agent."
- **s. 184**: "As between the principal and third persons, any person may become an agent, but no person who is not of the age of majority and of sound mind can become an agent, so as to be responsible to his principal according to the provisions in that behalf herein contained."
  - Observation for the paper: the Privy Council in Mohori Bibee (para 16) summarised ss. 183-184 as "no person under the age of majority can employ or be an agent". The text of s. 184 is narrower: a minor can be an agent as between principal and third persons; what he cannot be is *responsible to his principal*. Confirmed against the printed text; whether later courts follow the text or the paraphrase was not checked.
- **ss. 247 and 248** (minor admitted to the benefits of partnership; liability on majority) are shown in the same PDF as "Rep. by s. 73 and the Second Schedule" of the Partnership Act 1932. So the Contract Act sections that Mohori Bibee relied on for the minor-partner point were replaced by Partnership Act s. 30.

## 2. Indian Majority Act 1875
Copy: `ic_wb_majority_20240331.pdf` (capture 31 Mar 2024). Read on official source via Internet Archive.

- **s. 3** (as substituted by Act 33 of 1999, s. 4, w.e.f. 16-12-1999): "(1) Every person domiciled in India shall attain the age of majority on his completing the age of eighteen years and not before. (2) In computing the age of any person, the day on which he was born is to be included as a whole day and he shall be deemed to have attained majority at the beginning of the eighteenth anniversary of that day."
- **Settles the pack's open point**: the 21-year limb is gone. The PDF's footnotes show s. 3 and old s. 4 were replaced by Act 33 of 1999 with effect from 16 December 1999.
- **s. 2 (Saving)**: "Nothing herein contained shall affect: (a) the capacity of any persons to act in the following matters (namely),--marriage, dower, divorce and adoption; (b) the religion or religious rites and usages of any class of citizens of India; or (c) ..." So personal-law capacity in those four matters is outside the Act.

## 3. Indian Partnership Act 1932, s. 30
Copy: `ic_wb_partnership_20250901.pdf` (capture 1 Sep 2025). Read on official source via Internet Archive.

- s. 30(1): "A person who is a minor according to the law to which he is subject may not be a partner in a firm, but, with the consent of all the partners for the time being, he may be admitted to the benefits of partnership."
- s. 30(2): the minor "has a right to such share of the property and of the profits of the firm as may be agreed upon, and he may have access to and inspect and copy any of the accounts of the firm."
- s. 30(3): "Such minor's share is liable for the acts of the firm, but the minor is not personally liable for any such act."
- s. 30(4): the minor "may not sue the partners for an account or payment of his share ... save when severing his connection with the firm".
- s. 30(5): within six months of attaining majority or of knowing of admission, whichever is later, the person "may give public notice that he has elected to become or that he has elected not to become a partner in the firm"; if he fails to give notice "he shall become a partner in the firm on the expiry of the said six months."
- s. 30(6): burden of proving lack of knowledge lies on those asserting it. s. 30(7)-(9): consequences of electing to become / not to become a partner; "Nothing in sub-sections (7) and (8) shall affect the provisions of section 28."

## 4. Limited Liability Partnership Act 2008 and Rules 2009
Act copy: `ic_wb_llp_20240814.pdf` (capture 14 Aug 2024, shows amendments to 2022). Read on official source via Internet Archive. Keyword search of the whole Act text for "minor", "infant" and "age of" returned **nothing**.

- **s. 4**: "Save as otherwise provided, the provisions of the Indian Partnership Act, 1932 (9 of 1932) shall not apply to a limited liability partnership." (So Partnership Act s. 30 does not carry over.)
- **s. 5**: "Any individual or body corporate may be a partner in a limited liability partnership: Provided that an individual shall not be capable of becoming a partner of a limited liability partnership, if-- (a) he has been found to be of unsound mind by a Court of competent jurisdiction and the finding is in force; (b) he is an undischarged insolvent; or (c) he has applied to be adjudicated as an insolvent and his application is pending."
- **s. 7(1)**: "Every limited liability partnership shall have at least two designated partners who are individuals and at least one of them shall be a resident in India". Explanation: "resident in India" = stayed in India "not less than one hundred and twenty days during the financial year".
- **s. 7(3)**: "An individual shall not become a designated partner in any limited liability partnership unless he has given his prior consent to act as such to the limited liability partnership in such form and manner as may be prescribed."
- **s. 7(4)**: particulars of every consenting individual to be filed with the registrar "within thirty days of his appointment".
- **s. 7(5)**: "An individual eligible to be a designated partner shall satisfy such conditions and requirements as may be prescribed."
- **s. 7(6)**: "Every designated partner of a limited liability partnership shall obtain a Designated Partner Identification Number (DPIN) from the Central Government and the provisions of sections 153 to 159 (both inclusive) of the Companies Act, 2013 shall apply mutatis mutandis for the said purpose." (So the DIN machinery, and with it the DIR-3 instruction kit, is imported for DPINs - an inference about the kit; the kit itself is about DIN.)
- **s. 11(1)(a)**: "two or more persons associated for carrying on a lawful business with a view to profit shall subscribe their names to an incorporation document"; **s. 11(2)(e)-(f)**: the document must "state the name and address of each of the persons who are to be partners ... on incorporation" and of the designated partners.
- **LLP Rules 2009 as notified** (G.S.R. 229(E), 1 April 2009; capture 24 Dec 2012: `mca_wb_LLPRules_notified.pdf`). Read on official source via Internet Archive. Searched for "minor", "eighteen", "years of age", "age of": no age rule. Rule 10 (DPIN) requires a Form 7 with a date of birth, with photo, proof of identity and residence; guideline 5 on the form: "The proof of identify enclosed with DPIN Form should also contain the Date of Birth of the applicant and the same should match the Date of Birth filled in the application form." Form 4 (partner details) also asks for date of birth. **Later amendments to the Rules were not read**, so the conclusion is limited to the 2009 text.
- **MCA FAQ, "What are the qualifications for becoming a partner?"** (capture 10 Jun 2023): repeats the three s. 5 disqualifications; on DPIN: "Enabling provisions have been made to prescribe under rules conditions, which would have to be fulfilled by an individual who is eligible to be appointed as a 'designated-partner'." The FAQ is silent on minors. Read on official source via Internet Archive (`mca_wb_faq_partners_20230610.txt`). This closes the pack's "MCA FAQ not readable" gap.
- Net: the LLP Act and the 2009 Rules say nothing about minors; the statutory route for "admitted to the benefits" is cut off by s. 4; the only imported age-adjacent barrier is the DIN/DPIN process.

## 5. Companies Act 2013
Copy: `ic_wb_companies_20241216.pdf` (India Code PDF, capture 16 Dec 2024). Read on official source via Internet Archive.

- **s. 153**: "Every individual intending to be appointed as director of a company shall make an application for allotment of Director Identification Number to the Central Government in such form and manner and along with such fees as may be prescribed" (with a proviso allowing another prescribed number to count as a DIN).
- **s. 154**: Central Government to allot a DIN "within one month from the receipt of the application".
- **s. 164(1)** (extract): "A person shall not be eligible for appointment as a director of a company, if -- (a) he is of unsound mind and stands so declared by a competent court; (b) he is an undischarged insolvent; (c) he has applied to be adjudicated as an insolvent and his application is pending; (d) he has been convicted by a court of any offence ... [imprisonment of not less than six months] ...; (e) an order disqualifying him ...; (f) he has not paid any calls ...; (g) ... section 188 ...; (h) he has not complied with sub-section (3) of section 152. [(i) ... section 165(1)]". **No age ground.** s. 164(3): "A private company may by its articles provide for any disqualifications for appointment as a director in addition to those specified in sub-sections (1) and (2)."
- **s. 196(3)(a)**: "No company shall appoint or continue the employment of any person as managing director, whole-time director or manager who -- (a) is below the age of twenty-one years or has attained the age of seventy years" (with provisos for 70+).
- **s. 109(4)** (nomination): "Where the nominee is a minor, it shall be lawful for the holder of the securities, making the nomination to appoint, in the prescribed manner, any person to become entitled to the securities of the company, in the event of the death of the nominee during his minority." The Act contemplates a minor holding securities by nomination.
- Keyword search of the Act for "minor": only s. 109(4) (above), "minority shareholding" provisions (s. 236) and an accounting standard reference. The Act has no general rule on minors as members.

## 6. Companies (Appointment and Qualification of Directors) Rules 2014
- Rule 9(1), as reproduced in the MCA's DIR-3 kit (2014 and 2023 captures; read on official source via Internet Archive): "Every individual, who is to be appointed as director of a company shall make an application electronically in Form No. DIR-3, to the Central Government for the allotment of a Director Identification Number (DIN) along with such fees as provided in the Companies (Registration Offices and Fees) Rules, 2014."
- Form DIR-3 fields (pre-notification chapter text, `mca_wb_NCARules_Ch11_2014.txt`): name, father's name, date of birth, nationality, PAN etc.; the draft form text carries no age statement. The age sentence lives in the instruction kit, not the form or rule.
- Notified Rules and the current DIR-3/SPICe forms: **not read**.

## 7. Hindu Minority and Guardianship Act 1956
Copy: `ic_wb_hmga_20220812.pdf` (capture 12 Aug 2022). Read on official source via Internet Archive.

- **s. 4(a)**: "'minor' means a person who has not completed the age of eighteen years".
- **s. 6**: natural guardians of a Hindu minor "in respect of the minor's person as well as in respect of the minor's property (excluding his or her undivided interest in joint family property)" are, for a boy or unmarried girl, "the father, and after him, the mother".
- **s. 8(1)**: "The natural guardian of a Hindu minor has power, subject to the provisions of this section, to do all acts which are necessary or reasonable and proper for the benefit of the minor or for the realization, protection or benefit of the minor's estate; but the guardian can in no case bind the minor by a personal covenant."
- **s. 8(2)**: not without previous court permission to "(a) mortgage or charge, or transfer by sale, gift, exchange or otherwise, any part of the immovable property of the minor; or (b) lease any part of such property for a term exceeding five years or for a term extending more than one year beyond the date on which the minor will attain majority."
- **s. 8(3)**: disposal in contravention "is voidable at the instance of the minor or any person claiming under him." **s. 8(4)**: court permission only "in case of necessity or for an evident advantage to the minor."
- The Act speaks of acts "for the benefit of the minor" and "the minor's estate"; it does not mention carrying on a business. Whether a guardian may carry on a business for a minor under s. 8(1) was not answered by any text read.
- The Act applies to Hindus (s. 3, not quoted). Other communities: Guardians and Wards Act (below) and personal law - not read.

## 8. Guardians and Wards Act 1890
Copy: `ic_wb_gwa_20260426.pdf` (capture 26 Apr 2026). Read on official source via Internet Archive.

- **s. 4(1)**: "'minor' means a person who, under the provisions of the Indian Majority Act, 1875, is to be deemed not to have attained his majority"; **s. 4(2)**: "'guardian' means a person having the care of the person of a minor or of his property, or of both his person and property".
- **s. 27**: "A guardian of the property of a ward is bound to deal therewith as carefully as a man of ordinary prudence would deal with it if it were his own, and, subject to the provisions of this Chapter, he may do all acts which are reasonable and proper for the realisation, protection or benefit of the property."
- **s. 29** (court-appointed guardians): not without previous court permission to mortgage, charge or transfer immovable property of the ward or lease it beyond five years / one year past majority. **s. 30**: a disposal in contravention "is voidable at the instance of any other person affected thereby." **s. 31(1)**: permission only "in case of necessity or for an evident advantage to the ward."
- s. 28 (testamentary guardians) read; its text is about restrictions on disposing of immovable property.

## 9. Related text read while checking (for lawyer-check.md)
- **Child and Adolescent Labour (Prohibition and Regulation) Act 1986** (India Code capture 6 Feb 2026, `ic_wb_childlabour_20260206.pdf`; read on official source via Internet Archive): s. 2(i) "adolescent" = completed 14th year but not 18th; s. 2(ii) "child" = under 14 "or such age as may be specified in the Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education Act, 2009, whichever is more"; s. 3(1): "No child shall be employed or permitted to work in any occupation or process." s. 3(2)(a): not applicable where the child "helps his family or family enterprise, which is other than any hazardous occupations or processes set forth in the Schedule, after his school hours or during vacations"; (b) artist in audio-visual entertainment (subject to conditions). Explanation: "family enterprises" = "any work, profession, manufacture or business which is performed by the members of the family with the engagement of other persons". s. 3A concerns adolescents in hazardous occupations (heading only read).
- **MCA Form MGT-1 (register of members)**, on a taxguru.in copy (secondary): fields "In case member is a minor, Name of guardian ... Date of birth of minor". Official Rules not read.
