## Part 1. Singapore

### 1.1 Age of majority is 21 - what supports it
- **Official statement, read on official source (Ministry of Law website):**
  - Second reading speech on the Civil Law (Amendment) Bill, Assoc Prof Ho Peng Kee, Senior Minister of State, **19 January 2009**: "Sir, the current minimum age of contractual capacity is tied to the age of majority, which is 21 years in Singapore." The speech also states the Bill "seeks to de-link the age of majority from the age of contractual capacity". URL: https://www.mlaw.gov.sg/news/parliamentary-speeches/second-reading-speech-on-civil-law-amendment-bill-by-senior-minister-of-state-assoc-prof-ho-peng/
  - Ministry press release "Proposal to lower the Age of Contractual Capacity from 21 years to 18 years, and the Civil Law (Amendment) Bill", **25 August 2008**: "The current minimum age of contractual capacity is tied to the age of majority, which is 21 years in Singapore." URL: https://www.mlaw.gov.sg/news/press-releases/proposal-to-lower-the-age-of-contractual-capacity-from-21-years-to-18-years-and-the-civil-law/
  - (The quotations were returned by a page-reading tool that limits quotation length; re-read both pages before quoting in the paper.)
- **Why the reform was made, same sources (relevant to the paper):** the speech says "the legal barriers preventing young people from starting and conducting a business should be removed" and "the current laws place unnecessary restrictions on young people wishing to do business"; the press release says the Pro-Enterprise Panel "has received feedback that the legal barriers preventing young people from starting and conducting a business should be removed" and that "The Government agrees that the current laws place unnecessary restriction on young people wishing to undertake business, and should be revised as part of the broader efforts to support an entrepreneurial society." Land is excluded: persons below 21 "are not able to hold land in their own name or sell their beneficial interests in land"; the reform keeps land contracts out except leases not exceeding 3 years (speech).
- **Statutory source for "21": not found.** No section of an Act setting the age of majority at 21 was located. The Interpretation Act 1965 and Application of English Law Act were opened on SSO only as shell pages (no text); the Interpretation Act definitions of "minor" and "infant" are therefore **not read**. What is read: Civil Law Act 1909 s. 35 (pack, read on SSO): a contract by "a minor who has attained the age of 18 years shall have effect as if he were of full age", which presupposes that 18-20 year-olds are still minors. So: "21" is supported by government statements in 2008-09 and by the structure of s. 35, not by a located statute. Treat the statutory basis as an open point for a Singapore lawyer.
- Minors' Contracts Act (Singapore), 1994 Revised Edition, informal consolidation "version in force from 20/5/1994" (read on official source via Internet Archive; `sso_wb_MCA1987_2020RevEd.pdf` despite its file name): s. 1 disapplies the Infants Relief Act 1874 for contracts after 9 April 1987; s. 2 (guarantees: a guarantee of an obligation "unenforceable against him (or he repudiates the contract) because he was a minor" is not for that reason unenforceable against the guarantor); s. 3(1) (restitution): "the court may, if it is just and equitable to do so, require the defendant to transfer to the plaintiff any property acquired by the defendant under the contract, or any property representing it"; s. 4 (contracts before 12 November 1993); s. 5 (short title). Its footnote: the Act was made applicable on 12 November 1993 by the Application of English Law Act. **Whether this edition is current was not checked.** This fills the pack's "headings only" gap.

### 1.2 ACRA's "18" - what statute is behind it
ACRA's published statements (pack, read on official source; pages "Last updated on 29 January 2026"): sole proprietor and partnership registration "Age: 18 years or above"; limited partnership limited partners "Must be at least 18 years old", local manager "Age: 18 years or above"; LLP "Individuals must be at least 18 years old"; company directors "Be 18 years old or older".

| Business form | ACRA guidance | Statute text found | Label |
|---|---|---|---|
| Company director | 18 | Companies Act 1967 s. 145(2): "No person other than a natural person who has attained the age of 18 years and who is otherwise of full legal capacity may be a director of a company." [7/2009] | official, SSO (pack) |
| Sole proprietor | 18 | **None for the proprietor.** Business Names Registration Act 2014 (2020 Rev Ed; version in force from 26 Feb 2024) contains an age only for the **authorised representative**, s. 11(2): "The authorised representative must be -- (a) a natural person; (b) at least 18 years of age; (c) otherwise of full legal capacity; and (d) ordinarily resident in Singapore." Section 5 (must register before carrying on business) and s. 9 (grounds on which the Registrar must refuse: unlawful purpose or prejudicial to public peace; national security or interest; foreign company not registered) contain no age ground. | official via IA |
| Partnership (business names) | 18 | Same: none for the partner; age only for the authorised representative (s. 11(2)). | official via IA |
| LLP partner | 18 | **None for the partner.** Limited Liability Partnerships Act 2005 (2020 Rev Ed; capture of version valid 23 Jul 2024), s. 11(1): "Any individual or body corporate may be a partner in a limited liability partnership." | official via IA |
| LLP manager | (not separately in guidance read) | s. 29(1): "Every limited liability partnership must ensure that it has at least one manager who -- (a) is a natural person; (b) has attained 18 years of age and is otherwise of full legal capacity; and (c) is ordinarily resident in Singapore." [7/2009] | official via IA |
| Limited partnership (general partner, limited partner, local manager) | 18 | **None found.** Limited Partnerships Act 2008 (Acts Supplement text as enacted; amendments not applied): keyword search for "attained", "age of", "years of age", "minor" found nothing; s. 28 local manager has no age; "individual" defined as a natural person. | official via IA |

- **Finding:** in the Singapore texts read, the 18-year requirement is written into the Companies Act (director), the LLP Act (manager) and the Business Names Registration Act (authorised representative). The statutes read do **not** state an age for a sole proprietor, a business-names partner, an LLP partner or a limited partner. ACRA's "18" for those roles is therefore either (a) in subsidiary legislation not obtained (Business Names Registration Regulations 2015; LLP Regulations; Limited Partnerships Regulations - SSO pages are shells in the archive), (b) an application of general contract capacity (a person under 18 has no contractual capacity under Civil Law Act s. 35, and registration is a contractual/legal act), or (c) administrative practice. Which of these is correct was **not determined**.
- Caution: the SSO copies are versions dated 2024 (BNRA 26 Feb 2024; LLP Act valid 23 Jul 2024). Amendments since then were not checked; re-read on SSO live.
- Another check made: Business Names Registration Act has a section restricting undischarged bankrupts carrying on business (s. 34) and Limited Partnerships Act s. 29 / LLP Act s. 58 for bankrupt managers; these are the only personal-eligibility limits besides the age rules above.

### 1.3 Singapore points useful for the table (read on official source unless stated)
- Singpass (the log-in used for ACRA, IRAS and IPOS e-services) has a minimum age of 15 for citizens, PRs and FIN holders - official Singpass support wording reported by search (https://portal.singpass.gov.sg/home/ui/support and ask.gov.sg answers); not opened in full. A 15-17 year old can hold a Singpass but, on ACRA's guidance, cannot register a business.
- No Singapore case on minors in business was located (pack and this session).

## Part 2. UK (England and Wales): can a minor be a member of an LLP?

### 2.1 What the statutes say
- **Limited Liability Partnerships Act 2000** (read on official source via Internet Archive, pack): s. 2(1)(a): an LLP is incorporated if "two or more persons associated for carrying on a lawful business with a view to profit" have subscribed their names to an incorporation document (wording as the pack records it; re-check). s. 4(2): "Any other person may become a member of a limited liability partnership by and in accordance with an agreement with the existing members." s. 4(2A) makes this subject to the bar on disqualified persons. **No minimum age appears in s. 2 or s. 4.**
- **The Limited Liability Partnerships (Application of Companies Act 2006) Regulations 2009, SI 2009/1804** (read on official source via Internet Archive; captures dated Oct-Nov 2025): the contents list for "Part 5 An LLP's members" shows only "17ZC Disqualified member" and "17B Duty to ensure disqualified person is not a member" as eligibility provisions; no provision applying the director minimum age (Companies Act 2006 s. 157) to members. The register of members must record, for an individual member, "(a) name and any former name; (b) a service address; (c) the country or state ... in which he is usually resident; (d) date of birth; (e) whether he is a designated member" (as applied s. 163; the register entry presupposes any age). **Only Part 5, the contents list and regulation 18's page were read; the rest of the Regulations were not read**, so "no minimum age anywhere in the Regulations" is not established.
- **Companies House guidance**, "Limited liability partnerships: incorporation and names" (GOV.UK, content last updated 1 February 2026; read on official source), section 2.3 "Who can be a member": "It's up to the members to decide who should be appointed to the LLP. They must not: have been disqualified from acting as a company director or member of an LLP (unless the court has given them permission to act for a particular LLP); be an un-discharged bankrupt (unless the court has given them permission to act for a particular LLP). All LLP members must verify their identity to prove who they are." No age is stated.
- **Companies House identity verification guidance** ("Verifying your identity for Companies House", GOV.UK, updated 1 June 2026): searched for age, "16", "under 18", "child", "minor": nothing found.
- **Directors, for contrast:** Companies House blog "Being 16: the minimum age of a company director" (12 December 2019): "A company must have at least one director who is 16 or over and not disqualified from being a director." (The post does not cite the section; the statute is Companies Act 2006 s. 157, pack.) A reply in the comments by a Companies House digital content designer (30 December 2019): "There is no minimum age limit for shareholders imposed within the Companies Act 2006." and "A company may however choose to add its own stipulations surrounding shareholder ages to its articles of association." Neither concerns LLP members.

### 2.2 What follows (and what does not)
- **Text:** no statutory minimum age for a member of an English LLP appears in LLPA 2000 ss. 2 and 4, in the parts of SI 2009/1804 read, or in Companies House's own guidance. Membership arises "by and in accordance with an agreement" (s. 4(2)).
- **General law fills the gap (not a statutory statement):** a minor's agreement is not void in England; the pack records the Minors' Contracts Act 1987 and the Family Law Reform Act 1969 s. 1 (age 18). The English rule that an infant partner is not personally liable for firm debts and cannot be made bankrupt as a partner is shown by Lovell and Christmas v Beauchamp [1894] AC 607, read only through the Calcutta High Court's account (cases.md, section E). I did not find a post-1987 English case or Companies House statement on a minor LLP member.
- **Contrast with India:** English statute-plus-guidance is silent, and silence is read through a voidable-contract baseline; Indian LLP law is silent, and silence is read through a void-contract baseline (Contract Act ss. 10-11; Mohori Bibee; Mathai Mathai). This is an inference from the texts read, not a finding in any source.
- **Scotland:** the Age of Legal Capacity (Scotland) Act 1991 gives capacity at 16 (pack); how that interacts with LLP membership was not checked.
- **Not found:** any HM Government or Companies House statement that a person under 16 (or 18) may or may not be an LLP member. A form LL IN01 (version 12.0 / 11.0, GOV.UK) was located by search but not read; it asks for each proposed member's date of birth.
