## Verdict (short)
- **Written already, and must be cited:** the void-agreement rule and its critique (Swaminathan and Surana 2021; Cheong, Dhankar and Star 2023; Star and Dhankar 2022; Gangwar 2022); a Shodhganga PhD thesis on "Rights and liabilities of minors in Indian legal system with special reference to commercial transactions" (Sarkar); and a Shodhganga PhD thesis on "Socio legal status of a minor in a partnership firm" (Dash, Sambalpur University, 2012, 291 pages). So the ordinary-partnership layer (Partnership Act s. 30) is **not** a new topic.
- **Touched only in passing:** whether a minor can be an LLP partner. One line in a 2018 SCC Online blog article lists it as an unresolved issue; one 2020 practitioner table asserts "In LLP minors cannot become partners" without citing a provision. Neither examines the LLP Act, the Rules, DPIN or the MCA's forms. This **narrows** the pack's statement that nothing was found on minors and the LLP Act: there is a one-line mention, not an analysis.
- **Not found anywhere searched:** (a) any analysis of the LLP Act's silence set against Partnership Act s. 30; (b) any source that traces the 18-year DIN rule to the MCA's DIR-3 instruction kit (the practitioner notes cite no provision); (c) any comparison of India, the UK, the US and Singapore on the acts a founder must perform; (d) any article or case on minors as directors under the 2013 Act; (e) any NCLT/NCLAT holding on a minor as member.
- **Limits of this verdict:** paid databases (SCC Online, Manupatra, HeinOnline, Westlaw), Shodhganga's thesis contents (blocked by a bot check), SSRN's search page (blocked), LiveLaw and Bar and Bench search (JavaScript pages that returned no results to a plain fetch) and several journals' own search tools were not usable. ## Swaminathan and Surana, "Minors' Contracts: A Major Problem with the Indian Contract Act, 1872"
- Statute Law Review 42(1), pp. 101-115 (OUP). DOI 10.1093/slr/hmy034. ISSN 0144-3593. Jindal Global University repository record https://pure.jgu.edu.in/id/eprint/2746/ (year shown there: 2021; first published online 2018 per the pack).
- **Open copy: none.** The repository lists the published PDF (209 kB) as "Restricted to Repository staff only" with a "Request a copy" button; no SSRN, Academia or ResearchGate copy appeared. So only the abstract could be read. The bullets below are the abstract, not the article's full argument:
  - Section 10 makes agreements enforceable when made with free consent of parties competent to contract; section 11 says minors are not competent.
  - The Act sets out the consequences of vitiated consent in sections 19, 19A and 20 but "omits spelling out the consequences of contracting with a minor".
  - The Privy Council in Mohori Bibee v Dharmodas Ghose (1903) read the Act as giving a definitive answer and treated minors' contracts as void ab initio ("not voidable or void").
  - On that view neither party can enforce such a contract, and neither can seek restitution under the provisions for voidable (s. 64) or void (s. 65) contracts.
  - The title ("a major problem") signals a critique of that result. What the authors propose, and how they treat guardians, agency, partnership or companies, is **not known** from the abstract.
- A search summary of Cheong, Dhankar and Star (OUCLJ 2023) says it cites Swaminathan and Surana and states that the apex courts of India and Malaysia both follow the void ab initio position (snippet only; check the article).
- Ready action: write to the corresponding author via the repository's "Request a copy", or ask a school/public library for an inter-library copy.

## Final verdict on what has and has not been written
1. **Written:** minors' contracts in India (void rule; Mohori Bibee; Mathai Mathai; reform arguments), comparisons with England and with sports/entertainment contracts, online contracts; two Shodhganga theses on minors in commercial transactions and in partnership firms. 
2. **Mentioned, not analysed:** minors and LLPs (SCC Times 2018, one line; iPleaders 2020, one table cell). Minors as shareholders appears in practitioner notes that disagree with each other (BnB Legal says no; CLB Padival says yes through a guardian).
3. **Not found:** an analysis of the LLP Act against Partnership Act s. 30; an account of the DIN age rule's source; a founder-act comparison across the four jurisdictions; any decision of NCLT/NCLAT on a minor's capacity to be a member; a free copy of the Swaminathan and Surana argument beyond its abstract.
4. **Consequence for the claim:** a claim of "first systematic treatment of minors across the business forms" is defensible only as "I did not find an earlier one", and the LLP point should be framed as "raised in passing before, not analysed". The Dash (2012) and Sarkar theses must be opened before any statement about what Indian theses cover.
