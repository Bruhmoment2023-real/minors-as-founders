## A. Age of majority and contractual capacity

### A1. Family Law Reform Act 1969, s. 1 [O-IA]
https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1969/46/section/1
- s. 1(1): "As from the date on which this section comes into force a person shall attain full age on attaining the age of eighteen instead of on attaining the age of twenty-one".
- s. 9(1) (https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1969/46/section/9): "The time at which a person attains a particular age expressed in years shall be the commencement of the relevant anniversary of the date of his birth."

### A2. Minors' Contracts Act 1987 [O-IA]
https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1987/13
- s. 1: "The following enactments shall not apply to any contract made by a minor after the commencement of this Act - (a) the Infants Relief Act 1874 (which invalidates certain contracts made by minors and prohibits actions to enforce contracts ratified after majority)..."
- s. 2: where a guarantee is given for a minor's contractual obligation and "the obligation is unenforceable against him (or he repudiates the contract) because he was a minor when the contract was made, the guarantee shall not for that reason alone be unenforceable against the guarantor."
- s. 3(1): where a contract "is unenforceable against the defendant (or he repudiates it) because he was a minor when the contract was made, the court may, if it is just and equitable to do so, require the defendant to transfer to the plaintiff any property acquired by the defendant under the contract, or any property representing it."
- Extent shown on the page: England and Wales.
Importance: the Act does not define which contracts bind a minor; it removes the 1874 Act (so ratification after 18 is effective again), makes guarantees of minors' obligations enforceable, and gives the court a restitution power. s. 2 is the statutory basis for the common commercial workaround of an adult guarantor.

### A3. Sale of Goods Act 1979, s. 3 [O-IA]
https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1979/54/section/3
- s. 3(2): "Where necessaries are sold and delivered to a minor or to a person who by reason of ... drunkenness is incompetent to contract, he must pay a reasonable price for them."
- s. 3(3): ""necessaries" means goods suitable to the condition in life of the minor or other person concerned and to his actual requirements at the time of the sale and delivery."
(The page shows amendments: the minor wording repealed for Scotland in 1991; the mental-incapacity wording ceased for England and Wales in 2007. Quote from the live page.)

### A4. Proform Sports Management Ltd v Proactive Sports Management Ltd [2006] EWHC 2903 (Ch), 26 July 2006 [S]
https://www.bailii.org/ew/cases/EWHC/Ch/2006/2903.html (read through an Internet Archive capture)
- The judge quotes a textbook passage: "On the other hand there is no general principle to the effect that any contract beneficial to a minor is binding on him. So a minor's trading contracts are not binding on him, even if beneficial." (The textbook is named in the judgment; the name and the paragraph number of the judgment were not recorded - check.)
- Holding on the class of binding contracts: "In my judgment, cases such as Chaplin and Doyle make it clear that the basis of the class of analogous contracts is that the minor is entitled to earn his living or to start to do so. It does not seem to me that the Proform agreement is analogous to such a contract."
- On inducing breach: "it is no tort to procure the breach of a voidable contract, at least where the person induced is the party wh..." (my extract stops mid-word here; read the full paragraph before quoting).
Importance: a modern English decision treating a 15-year-old's management agreement as voidable, and repeating that a minor's **trading** contracts do not bind him even if beneficial. That sentence is the heart of the English position for a teenage founder.

## B. Company director

### B1. Companies Act 2006, s. 157 [O-IA]
https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2006/46/section/157
- s. 157(1): "A person may not be appointed a director of a company unless he has attained the age of 16 years."
- s. 157(2): "This does not affect the validity of an appointment that is not to take effect until the person appointed attains that age."
- s. 157(4): "An appointment made in contravention of this section is void."
- s. 157(5): "Nothing in this section affects any liability of a person under any provision of the Companies Acts if he - (a) purports to act as director, or (b) acts as a shadow director, although he could not, by virtue of this section, be validly appointed as a director."
- s. 157(6): "This section has effect subject to section 158 (power to provide for exceptions from minimum age requirement)."

### B2. Companies Act 2006, s. 158 [O-IA]
https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2006/46/section/158
- s. 158(1): "The Secretary of State may make provision by regulations for cases in which a person who has not attained the age of 16 years may be appointed a director of a company."
- The page records amendments by the Economic Crime and Corporate Transparency Act 2023, s. 41, in force 4 March 2024. Whether any regulations have ever been made under s. 158 was not checked.

### B3. Companies Act 2006, s. 159 [O-IA]
https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2006/46/section/159 - the page shows s. 159 (existing under-age directors) as "omitted (26.10.2023 for specified purposes, 4.3.2024 in so far as not already in force) by virtue of Economic Crime and Corporate Transparency Act 2023 (c. 56), ss. 41(4)". Do not cite s. 159 as current law.

### B4. The gap between 16 and 18
A 16- or 17-year-old may lawfully be a director (B1) but is still a minor for contract purposes (A1, A4). The director signs for the company, which has full capacity, so the company's contracts are unaffected; the director's **own** undertakings (personal guarantees, service agreement, bank mandate) remain voidable. This is an inference from the texts above, not a quoted authority; confirm with a lawyer.

## C. Shareholder

- **Companies Act 2006, s. 112** [O-IA] https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2006/46/section/112 : s. 112(1) "The subscribers of a company's memorandum are deemed to have agreed to become members of the company, and on its registration become members"; s. 112(2) "Every other person who agrees to become a member of a company, and whose name is entered in its register of members, is a member of the company." **No age condition appears.**

## E. Scotland: a different model inside the UK

**Age of Legal Capacity (Scotland) Act 1991** [O-IA]
- s. 1(1) https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1991/50/section/1 : "(a) a person under the age of 16 years shall, subject to section 2 below, have no legal capacity to enter into any transaction; (b) a person of or over the age of 16 years shall have legal capacity to enter into any transaction."
- s. 2(1) https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1991/50/section/2 : "A person under the age of 16 years shall have legal capacity to enter into a transaction - (a) of a kind commonly entered into by persons of his age and circumstances, and (b) on terms which are not unreasonable."
- s. 3(1) https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1991/50/section/3 : "A person under the age of 21 years ("the applicant") may make application to the court to set aside a transaction which he entered into while he was of or over the age of 16 years but under the age of 18 years and which is a prejudicial transaction."
- s. 3(2): a prejudicial transaction is one which "(a) an adult, exercising reasonable prudence, would not have entered into in the circumstances of the applicant at the time of entering into the transaction, and (b) has caused or is likely to cause substantial prejudice to the applicant."
- s. 3(3): "Subsection (1) above shall not apply to - ... (f) a transaction in the course of the applicant's trade, business or profession; (g) a transaction into which any other party was induced to enter by virtue of any fraudulent misrepresentation by the applicant as to age or other material fact; (h) a transaction ratified by the applicant after he attained the age of 18 years...; or (j) a transaction ratified by the court under section 4 below."
Importance: the clearest reform precedent found. At 16 a person has full capacity; a safety valve exists for prejudicial transactions; and **business transactions are expressly taken out of the safety valve**, so counterparties of a 16-year-old trader have certainty.
