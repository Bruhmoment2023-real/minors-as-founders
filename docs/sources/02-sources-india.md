## A. Capacity to contract

### A1. Indian Contract Act 1872 [O]
Source: India Code PDF, https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/2187/2/A187209.pdf

- **s. 10**: "All agreements are contracts if they are made by the free consent of parties competent to contract, for a lawful consideration and with a lawful object, and are not hereby expressly declared to be void."
- **s. 11**: "Every person is competent to contract who is of the age of majority according to the law to which he is subject, and who is of sound mind, and is not disqualified from contracting by any law to which he is subject."
- **s. 2(g)**: "An agreement not enforceable by law is said to be void".
- **s. 65**: "When an agreement is discovered to be void, or when a contract becomes void, any person who has received any advantage under such agreement or contract is bound to restore it, or to make compensation for it to the person from whom he received it." (No Explanation about minors was seen in the text read; see the Law Commission recommendation at A9.)
- **s. 68**: "If a person, incapable of entering into a contract, or any one whom he is legally bound to support, is supplied by another person with necessaries suited to his condition in life, the person who has furnished such supplies is entitled to be reimbursed from the property of such incapable person."
- **s. 183**: "Any person who is of the age of majority according to the law to which he is subject, and who is of sound mind, may employ an agent."
- **s. 184**: "As between the principal and third persons, any person may become an agent, but no person who is not of the age of majority and of sound mind can become an agent, so as to be responsible to his principal according to the provisions in that behalf herein contained."

Why ss. 183-184 matter for this paper: read together they say a minor cannot appoint an agent, but a minor can be one. A firm or company (the principal) may therefore act through a minor as its agent towards third parties; what is missing is the minor's liability to the principal. This is the textual basis for asking whether a minor can sign for a business he does not legally "run". It is an inference from the text, not a holding; a lawyer must confirm it.

### A3. Mohori Bibee v Dharmodas Ghose (1903) 30 IA 114 (PC) [N for the original report; S for the passages below]
The original report could not be opened on any free source reached (not found as a standalone document on Indian Kanoon; BAILII blocks automated access). The words below are the Privy Council's words **as quoted by the Supreme Court** in Mathai Mathai (A4):
- "Looking at these sections their Lordships are satisfied that the Act makes it essential that all contracting parties should be "competent to contract," and expressly provides that a person, who by reason of infancy is incompetent to contract, cannot make a contract within the meaning of the Act"
- "The question whether a contract is void or voidable presupposes the existence of a contract within the meaning of the Act..."
The Law Commission's summary of the holding is at A9. Sean must read the full report (a law library, or SCC Online/Manupatra through a school or university) before citing a page number.

### A4. Mathai Mathai v Joseph Mary @ Marykkutty Joseph, Supreme Court, Civil Appeal No. 4479 of 2007, 25 April 2014 (V. Gopala Gowda and Gyan Sudha Misra JJ) [S]
Source: https://indiankanoon.org/doc/173680209/
- Para 10: "As per the Indian Contract Act,1872 it is clearly stated that for an agreement to become a contract, the parties must be competent to contract, wherein age of majority is a condition for competency. A deed of mortgage is a contract and we cannot hold that a mortgage in the name of a minor is valid, simply because it is in the interests of the minor unless she is represented by her natural guardian or guardian appointed by the court."
- Same para: "The law cannot be read differently for a minor who is a mortgagor and a minor who is a mortgagee... Therefore, this Court has to hold that the mortgage deed-Ex.A1 is void ab initio in law and the appellant cannot claim any rights under it."
- The Court also said: "Many courts have held that a minor can be a mortgagee as it is transfer of property in the interest of the minor. We feel that this is an erroneous application of the law..."
Importance: the most recent Supreme Court statement found that a minor's own agreement is void even where the minor is the party who benefits, unless a guardian acts. It is in tension with A5. The reported citation (believed to be (2015) 5 SCC 622) was **not** verified.

### A5. A.T. Raghava Chariar v O.A. Srinivasa Raghava Chariar, Madras Full Bench, 5 April 1916, AIR 1917 Mad 630 [S]
Source: https://indiankanoon.org/doc/844813/
- The question referred was whether a mortgage in favour of a minor who had advanced the money is enforceable. Abdur Rahim OCJ: "In this case, does the question referred to us involve the enforcing of any contract made by the minor? In my opinion, it does not."
- The reasoning turns on ss. 10-11 speaking of the person **making the promise sought to be enforced**.
Importance: the older "a minor can be a promisee/transferee" line that Mathai Mathai called erroneous. The paper must present both and say which is binding (the Supreme Court). Only the passages quoted were read, not the whole judgment.

### A6. Suraj Narain Dube v Sukhu Aheer, Allahabad, 6 July 1928, AIR 1928 All 440 [S]

### A7. Restitution: Specific Relief Act 1963, s. 33 [S]
Source: https://indiankanoon.org/doc/172614/
- s. 33(1): "On adjudging the cancellation of an instrument, the court may require the party to whom such relief is granted, to restore, so far as may be any benefit which he may have received from the other party and to make any compensation to him which justice may require."
- s. 33(2)(b): where a defendant successfully resists a suit on the ground "that the agreement sought to be enforced against him in the suit is void by reason of his not having been competent to contract under section 11 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872 (9 of 1872), the court may, if the defendant has received any benefit under the agreement from the other party, require him to restore, so far as may be, such benefit to that party, to the extent to which he or his estate has benefited thereby."

### A9. Law Commission of India, 13th Report (Contract Act, 1872), paras 35-37 [S]
Source: reproduction at https://www.advocatekhoj.com/library/lawreports/indiancontractact/18.php (the official PDF was not located).
- Para 35 summarises Khan Gul (Shadi Lal CJ): a minor who lied about his age "is not estopped from pleading his infancy", but "a false representation by an infant that he was of full age, gives rise to an equitable liability".
- Para 36 summarises Ajudhia Prasad: "where the property is not traceable, the grant of compensation would be tantamount to enforcing a void contract under the cloak of an equitable doctrine."
- Para 36 summarises Mohori Bibee: "(i) The question whether a contract is void or voidable presupposes the existence of a contract and cannot arise in the case of an infant; (ii) sections 64 and 65 start from the basis of there being an agreement between competent parties".
- Para 37 (recommendation): "We feel that the Judicial Committee had not correctly interpreted section 65... We recommend that an Explanation be added to section 65 to indicate that section should be applicable where a minor enters into an agreement on the false representation that he is a major."
Importance: an existing Indian reform proposal, apparently not enacted (A1, s. 65). The report's year and official pagination were not verified.

### A10. Contracts made by a guardian

- **Mir Sarwarjan v Fakhruddin Mahomed Chowdhuri, Privy Council, 9 November 1911, (1912) 14 Bom LR 5** [S] https://indiankanoon.org/doc/1036373/ (Indian Kanoon files it under "Bombay High Court"; the text shows it is a Privy Council appeal from Calcutta). Para 7: "it is not within the competence of a manager of a minor's estate or within the competence of a guardian of a minor to bind the minor or the minor's estate by a contract for the purchase of immoveable property... as the minor in the present case was not bound by the contract there was no mutuality, and that the minor who has now reached his majority cannot obtain specific performance of the contract."
- **Sri Kakulam Subrahmanyam v Kurra Subba Rao, Privy Council, 26 February 1948, (1948) 50 Bom LR 646** [S] https://indiankanoon.org/doc/409754/ . Para 8: "It is clear that, if the mother and guardian had taken no part at all in the transaction, the respondent could not have entered into a valid contract to sell the land". Para 10 (quoting Pollock and Mulla with approval): a guardian's contract "can be specifically enforced by or against the minor, if the contract is one which it is within the competence of the guardian to enter into on his behalf so as to bind him by it, and, further, if it is for the benefit of the minor. But if either of these two conditions is wanting, the contract cannot be specifically enforced at all."
- **Raj Rani v Prem Adib, Bombay, 21 July 1948, AIR 1949 Bom 215** (Desai J) [S] https://indiankanoon.org/doc/1972437/ . Para 10: "under Section 183 of the Indian Contract Act, a minor is not entitled to employ an agent. The contract, therefore, though it is made for and on behalf of the minor by a person who purported to act as his agent, is not the contract of the minor." Para 12: "Under Section 11 the plaintiff was not competent to enter into a contract and, therefore, her promise would not be enforceable against her. In my opinion, therefore, the plaintiff's promise to serve supplies no consideration and the contract was therefore void." (OCR typos in the source corrected: "purp orted", "strve".)
  Importance: a service contract signed by a father for his minor daughter could not be enforced **by the minor**. This is the closest reported analogue to a parent signing a commercial contract "for" a teenage founder.

### A11. Guardians' statutory powers
- **Hindu Minority and Guardianship Act 1956, s. 8(1)** [S] https://indiankanoon.org/doc/137252432/ : "The natural guardian of a Hindu minor has power, subject to the provisions of this section, to do all acts which are necessary or reasonable and proper for the benefit of the minor or for the realisation, protection or benefit of the minor's estate; but the guardian can in no case bind the minor by a personal covenant." s. 8(2) requires court permission to "mortgage or charge, or transfer by sale, gift, exchange or otherwise, any part of the immovable property of the minor". The Act applies to Hindus as defined in it; guardians of minors of other communities fall under personal law and the 1890 Act. Not examined.
- **Guardians and Wards Act 1890, s. 27** [S] https://indiankanoon.org/doc/1538739/ : "A guardian of the property of a ward is bound to deal therewith as carefully as a man of ordinary prudence would deal with it, if it were his own, and, subject to the provisions of this Chapter, he may do all acts which are reasonable and proper for the realisation, protection or benefit of the property."

## B. Partnership

### B1. Indian Partnership Act 1932, s. 30 [S]
Source: https://indiankanoon.org/doc/1921150/
- s. 30(1): "A person who is a minor according to the law to which he is subject may not be a partner in a firm, but, with the consent of all the partners for the time being, he may be admitted to the benefits of partnership."
- s. 30(2): "Such minor has a right to such share of the property and of the profits of the firm as may be agreed upon, and he may have access to and inspect and copy any of the accounts of the firm."
- s. 30(3): "Such minor's share is liable for the acts of the firm, but the minor is not personally liable for any such act."
- s. 30(5): "At any time within six months of his attaining majority, or of his obtaining knowledge that he had been admitted to the benefits of partnership, whichever date is later, such person may give public notice that he has elected to become or that he has elected not to become a partner in the firm... Provided that, if he fails to give such notice, he shall become a partner in the firm on the expiry of the said six months."
- s. 30(7)(a): on becoming a partner "he also becomes personally liable to third parties for all acts of the firm done since he was admitted to the benefits of partnership".

### B2. Commissioner of Income-tax, Bombay v Dwarkadas Khetan & Co, Supreme Court, 1 December 1960, AIR 1961 SC 680, [1961] 2 SCR 821 [S]
Source: https://indiankanoon.org/doc/1662200/
- "Section 30 of the Indian Partnership Act clearly lays down that a minor cannot become a partner, though with the consent of the adult partners, he may be admitted to the benefits of partnership. Any document which goes beyond this section cannot be regarded as valid for the purpose of registration."
- Facts (headnote): the minor was "described as a full partner with equal rights and obligations with the other adult partners" and had signed the deed.

### B3. Commissioner of Income-tax v Shah Mohandas Sadhuram, Supreme Court, 15 April 1965, AIR 1966 SC 15, [1965] 3 SCR 771 [S]
Source: https://indiankanoon.org/doc/411335/
- "First it is clear from sub-s.(2) of s. 30 of the Partnership Act that a minor cannot be made liable for losses."
- "It appears to us that the guardian can do all that is necessary to effectuate the conferment and receipt of the benefits of partnership... as long as a partnership deed does not make a minor full partner a partnership deed cannot be regarded as invalid on the ground that a guardian has purported to contract on behalf of a minor."

### B4. Shivagouda Ravji Patil v Chandrakant Neelkanth Sedalge, Supreme Court, 8 May 1964, AIR 1965 SC 212, [1964] 8 SCR 233 [S, headnote only]
Source: https://indiankanoon.org/doc/91068/ . Headnote: a minor admitted to benefits of a firm that was dissolved before he came of age "cannot be adjudicated insolvent for the acts of insolvency of other partners".

### B5. Sanyasi Charan Mandal v Krishnadhan Banerji, Privy Council, 17 January 1922, (1922) 24 Bom LR 700 [S, partly read]
Source: https://indiankanoon.org/doc/1412894/ . On the pre-1932 law (Contract Act s. 247): "Under the section liability is limited to the share of the minor in the property of the firm."

### B6. Addepally Nageswara Rao & Brothers v CIT, Andhra Pradesh HC, 16 September 1969, [1971] 79 ITR 306 [S, partly read]
Source: https://indiankanoon.org/doc/1615453/ . A deed signed for a minor by his father who was also a partner; discusses s. 30(4). Read only in part; do not cite for a holding without reading it fully.

## C. Limited liability partnership

### C1. Limited Liability Partnership Act 2008 [S]
- **s. 5** https://indiankanoon.org/doc/175600484/ : "Any individual or body corporate may be a partner in a limited liability partnership: Provided that an individual shall not be capable of becoming a partner of a limited liability partnership, if- (a) he has been found to be of unsound mind by a Court of competent jurisdiction and the finding is in force; (b) he is an undischarged insolvent; or (c) he has applied to be adjudicated as an insolvent and his application is pending."
- **s. 7** https://indiankanoon.org/doc/49441206/ : s. 7(1) "Every limited liability partnership shall have at least two designated partners who are individuals and at least one of them shall be a resident in India". s. 7(3) "An individual shall not become a designated partner in any limited liability partnership unless he has given his prior consent to act as such". s. 7(5) "An individual eligible to be a designated partner shall satisfy such conditions and requirements as may be prescribed." s. 7(6) "Every designated partner of a limited liability partnership shall obtain a Designated Partner Identification Number (DPIN) from the Central Government and the provisions of sections 153 to 159 (both inclusive) of the Companies Act, 2013... shall apply mutatis mutandis for the said purpose."

**Finding (textual, to be confirmed):** s. 5 lists three disqualifications and minority is not one of them. The Act has no counterpart to s. 30 of the Partnership Act: nothing lets a minor be "admitted to the benefits" of an LLP, and nothing expressly excludes a minor. The exclusion in practice appears to come from s. 11 of the Contract Act (the LLP agreement is a contract), the consent requirement in s. 7(3), and the DPIN/DIN process. This statutory silence is the paper's most specific Indian gap.

## D. Company

### D1. Minor as subscriber or allottee: void
- **M.S. Palaniappa Mudaliar v Official Liquidator, Pasupathi Bank Ltd, Madras, 2 February 1942, AIR 1942 Mad 470** [S] https://indiankanoon.org/doc/1090788/ . "An application was made by M.S. Palaniappa Mudaliar as guardian of his minor daughter Meenakshi for shares and the company issued shares to and registered shares in the name of Meenakshi, describing her as a minor. This transaction was void on the face of it. An application for shares cannot be a necessity for a minor". The father could not be put on the list of contributories in her place. (OCR typo "guardias" corrected.)
- **Dalmia Jain Airways Ltd v Saroj Rani, Punjab HC, 24 March 1954, AIR 1956 Punj 41** (Kapur J) [S] https://indiankanoon.org/doc/989434/ . Follows Palaniappa: where a father applies for shares in a minor's name and the company registers the minor as such, "the transaction is void on the face of it and the father of the minor who has signed the application cannot be deemed to have contracted for the shares".
  Note: both arose under the 1913 Act and concerned partly-paid shares or liquidation. Whether they govern fully-paid shares today is a question for a lawyer.

### D2. Minor as transferee of fully paid shares through a guardian [N]
Commonly cited: **Diwan Singh v Minerva Films Ltd, AIR 1959 Punj 106**, and Department of Company Affairs clarifications. **None could be found or read.** Practitioner notes (01-prior-work.md, items 20-22) assert that a minor may hold fully paid shares acquired by gift or transfer with the guardian acting. Treat as unverified practice. A lawyer must supply the authority.
- Related, read: **Yash Vardhan Mall v Indrapuri Studios Pvt Ltd, NCLAT, Company Appeal (AT) No. 321 of 2019, 28 March 2023** [S, partly read] https://indiankanoon.org/doc/43029953/ - a dispute in which a company recorded the guardian of a minor nominee in its register of members. ## E. Banking
