## A. Age of majority and contractual capacity

### A1. Age of majority [N]
The scoping assumption is that the age of majority in Singapore is 21 at common law, with no general statute. **No primary source for "21" was read.** The only textual support found is indirect: s. 35 of the Civil Law Act speaks of "a minor who has attained the age of 18 years", which presupposes that 18-year-olds are still minors. The paper needs a cited authority (a Singapore judgment or an official statement) for 21.

### A2. Civil Law Act 1909, s. 35 [O]
https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/CLA1909?ProvIds=pr35-,pr36-
- s. 35(1): "Except as otherwise provided by this section or by any other written law, as from 1 March 2009, a contract entered into by a minor who has attained the age of 18 years shall have effect as if he were of full age."
- s. 35(3): subsection (1) does not affect "(a) a minor's lack of capacity to enter into contracts arising from reasons other than because of his age; or (b) the application of any provision in any other written law stipulating a requirement as to the attainment of a particular age for the purposes of that written law."
- s. 35(4): "The following contracts, if entered into by a minor who has attained the age of 18 years, shall not, by virtue of subsection (1), have effect as if he were of full age: (a) any contract for the sale, purchase, mortgage, assignment or settlement of any land, other than a contract for a lease of land not exceeding 3 years; (b) any contract for a lease of land for more than 3 years; (c) any contract whereby the minor's beneficial interest under a trust is sold or otherwise transferred to another person, or pledged as a collateral for any purpose; (d) any contract for the settlement of - (i) any legal proceedings or action in respect of which the minor is, pursuant to any written law, considered to be a person under disability on account of his age; or (ii) any claim from which any such legal proceedings or action may arise."
- s. 35(7): "Nothing in this section shall limit or affect the rule of law whereby a minor is not liable in tort for procuring a contract by means of fraudulent representations as to his own age or any other matter."
- The section is annotated "[7/2009]" (Act 7 of 2009).

### A3. Civil Law Act 1909, s. 36 [O]
- s. 36(1): "Notwithstanding any other written law, a minor who has attained the age of 18 years and who is not otherwise under any legal disability - (a) may, in his own name and without a litigation representative, bring, defend, conduct or intervene in any legal proceeding or action specified in the Schedule as if he were of full age".
The Schedule was not read.

Importance of A2-A3: Singapore lowered the age for **contracts** and for specified litigation to 18 in 2009 without changing the age of majority, and wrote the business purpose into the list of exceptions (land and trust interests stay protected). It shows that capacity can be granted for commerce separately from general adulthood. Below 18 the common law of minors' contracts still applies.

### A5. Sale of Goods Act 1979 (Singapore), s. 3 [O]
https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/SGA1979?ProvIds=pr3-
- s. 3(2): "Where necessaries are sold and delivered to a minor or to a person who by reason of mental incapacity or drunkenness is incompetent to contract, he must pay a reasonable price for them."
- s. 3(3): ""necessaries" means goods suitable to the condition in life of the minor or other person concerned and to his actual requirements at the time of the sale and delivery."

## B. Company director

**Companies Act 1967, s. 145** [O] https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/CoA1967?ProvIds=pr145-
- s. 145(1): "Every company must have at least one director who is ordinarily resident in Singapore and, where the company only has one member, that sole director may also be the sole member of the company."
- s. 145(2): "No person other than a natural person who has attained the age of 18 years and who is otherwise of full legal capacity may be a director of a company." Annotated "[7/2009]".

**ACRA guidance, "Choosing company directors & other key officers"** [O] https://www.acra.gov.sg/how-to-guides/setting-up-a-local-company/appointing-directors-company-secretary-and-other-key-personnel (page marked "Last updated on 29 January 2026"):
- "Under the Companies Act, company directors must: Be ordinarily resident; Be 18 years old or older; Be mentally fit to make decisions; ...Not be disqualified from acting as a director of a company".
  (ACRA's first bullet compresses s. 145(1), which requires at least one ordinarily resident director, not every director. Quote the statute for the rule.)

## C. Sole proprietorship, partnership, LP, LLP

## E. What Singapore contributes to the comparison
- An express statutory director age (18), like the UK (16) and New York (18), unlike Delaware and, on the text read, India.
- A single administrative statement of the age for every business form, which India lacks.
- A 2009 reform that separated contractual capacity (18) from majority, with a short list of protected transactions.
- The strictest result of the four for a 16-year-old: no route to register a business, be a partner, or be a director, on ACRA's published guidance.
