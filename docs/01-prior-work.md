# 01 - Prior work: annotated bibliography and novelty verdict

Searches run 13-14 October 2026. Research record only; not legal advice.

## Novelty verdict (provisional)

- **Well covered already:** the Indian rule that a minor's agreement is void, its history, and its comparison with English law. Swaminathan and Surana (item 1) is a direct, recent, peer-reviewed critique. Sean cannot present "the void rule is a problem" as his discovery.
- **Not found anywhere:** a paper that takes the acts a founder must perform (contract, partner, LLP partner, shareholder, director, bank account, tax registration, IP, online accounts) and tests each against Indian entity law, or that compares India, the UK, the US and Singapore on those acts. The closest thematic match is a Polish-language article on minor entrepreneurs under Polish law (item 6). Nothing was found on minors and the Indian LLP Act, on the absence of a statutory director age in India, or on the One Person Company bar.
- **So the gap is real but narrower than the scoping note said.** The contribution is the entity-law layer and the founder-act matrix, built on top of an existing contract-law literature that must be cited.
- **Why the verdict is still provisional:** the searches that could not be run are exactly the ones that cover Indian law journals (list at the end). A librarian-assisted search of SCC Online, Manupatra or HeinOnline, and a browser search of Shodhganga, are needed before the paper claims novelty in print.

## A. Closest scholarly work

1. **Shivprasad Swaminathan and Ragini Surana, "Minors' Contracts: A Major Problem with the Indian Contract Act, 1872", Statute Law Review 42(1), 101-115** (published online 2018). https://doi.org/10.1093/slr/hmy034 [abstract read, in part]
   - Abstract: s. 10 makes agreements by "competent" parties enforceable, s. 11 makes minors incompetent, but the Act "omits spelling out the consequences of contracting with a minor"; Mohori Bibee read the Act as making minors' contracts void ab initio.
   - Use: the leading modern critique of the void rule. Sean must read it in full (paywalled; request through a library or email the authors) and position his paper as extending it from contract doctrine to business forms.

2. **May Fong Cheong, Divyangana Dhankar and Shaun Star, "Legal transplantation of minors' contracts in India and Malaysia: 'Weak' Watson and a 'misfitted' transplant", Oxford University Commonwealth Law Journal 23(1), 17-43 (2023).** https://doi.org/10.1080/14729342.2023.2189359 [metadata only]
   - Use: comparative and historical treatment of how the English rule was transplanted and changed. Likely to cover Mohori Bibee in depth.

3. **Shaun Star and Divyangana Dhankar, "Major Differences in Minors' Contracts: A Comparative Analysis into the Validity of Contracts with Minors in the Sport and Entertainment Industry", Liverpool Law Review 43(2), 203-235 (2022).** https://doi.org/10.1007/s10991-022-09308-4 [abstract read]
   - Abstract: in Australia and the UK such contracts are generally voidable and beneficial service contracts bind the minor; "Indian courts do not prescribe to this view holding contracts with minors, void ab initio and unenforceable against contractual parties, with few exceptions."
   - Use: the nearest thing to a sector study. It does for young athletes and performers what Sean proposes for young founders. Cite as the model and distinguish.

4. **Shivangi Gangwar, "Minors' Contracts in the Digital Age", Liverpool Law Review 43(2), 237-261 (2022).** https://doi.org/10.1007/s10991-022-09298-3 [metadata only]
   - Use: online contracting by minors; relevant to the payment-gateway and platform section.

5. **Simon Goodfellow, "Who Gets the Better Deal?: A Comparison of the U.S. and English Infancy Doctrines", Hastings International and Comparative Law Review vol. 29 no. 1.** https://repository.uclawsf.edu/hastings_international_comparative_law_review/vol29/iss1/6 [abstract read; open access]
   - Abstract: compares the two doctrines and "concludes that a majority of American states strongly favor the interests of the minor". A search-engine summary adds that it points to Scotland as a model; that was not confirmed in the text.
   - Year: the repository lists vol. 29 under 2006; confirm from the PDF.

6. **Jakub Suchański, "Sytuacja prawna małoletnich przedsiębiorców na tle propozycji zmian w prawie" (reported English title: "Legal Situation of Minor Entrepreneurs Against the Background of Proposals for Changes in the Law"), a journal of Maria Curie-Skłodowska University, Lublin (2023).** https://journals.umcs.pl/szn/article/view/15909 [opened; Polish; abstract not extracted]
   - Use: the only work found whose subject is minor entrepreneurs as such. Polish law, including a draft law for minor business owners. Shows the question is live in at least one civil-law country. Journal name, volume and pages need confirming from the PDF.

7. **Hassiba Benkheda, "Between Protection And Challenges: What Legal Status For The Minor Partner In A General Partnership Company" (2024).** https://doi.org/10.37136/0515-016-002-014 [metadata only; Arabic-language journal]
   - Use: a minor as partner under (apparently) Algerian law. Evidence that the partnership question has been studied outside the common law.

8. **James Gilbert, "A Major Misunderstanding of Minors' Contracts? Enforcement and Restitution Under the Minors' Contracts Act 1969", Victoria University of Wellington Law Review 40(4), 721-742 (2009).** https://doi.org/10.26686/vuwlr.v40i4.5254 [abstract read; open access]
   - Abstract: the New Zealand Act divides minors' contracts into those "presumptively enforceable against the minor in the absence of unconscionability or oppression" and those presumptively unenforceable subject to judicial discretion.
   - Use: a fifth-jurisdiction reform model (a fairness-based statute). Useful in the reform section even though New Zealand is outside the four.

## D. Practitioner notes (evidence of practice, not authority)

20. **IndiaFilings, "Shareholding by minors in India".** https://www.indiafilings.com/learn/shareholding-by-minors-in-india [opened] - states that gifted shares are handled by the guardian, that "a minor cannot become a director of a company in India", and that a DIN "requires the person to be at least 18 years old". Cites no section for the age.
21. **CA B K Goyal & Co, "Can a shareholder/partner be minor".** https://www.cabkgoyal.com/can-a-shareholder-partner-be-minor/ [opened] - says of LLPs: "In the case of a minor, neither is it possible to enter into an agreement nor is practicable to hold a DPIN"; and that a DIN "can be obtained only after the person attains majority age". Cites no section.
22. **LegalWiz, "Who can be a director of a company?".** https://www.legalwiz.in/blog/who-can-be-a-company-director-in-india [opened] - "a DIN is also mandatory... which can only be obtained after you turn 18." Cites no section.
    - Items 20-22 agree with each other and none cites a provision for the 18-year DIN rule. That pattern is itself a finding: the rule everyone repeats could not be traced to a text (see 02-sources-india.md, D4).
