**Status words**
- **Allowed** - the text read permits it without an adult.
- **Through guardian** - permitted only if a guardian, custodian or adult representative acts.
- **Voidable** - the minor may do it, but may later escape it; the other side is bound.
- **Void** - of no legal effect from the start.
- **Barred** - a rule excludes minors.
- **Silent** - the text read sets no age; the answer depends on general contract law or on practice.
- **Not verified** - no source read.

**Confidence marks:** (O) official text read; (O-IA) official text read through an Internet Archive capture; (S) reliable secondary copy read; (N) not read - the cell rests on memory or practitioner notes and must not be relied on.

"US" means Delaware, with California and New York where stated. "UK" means England and Wales; Scotland is noted separately.

## Gaps and contradictions the table exposes

1. **India: the LLP Act forgot minors.** The 1932 Act has a full code for them (s. 30). The 2008 Act says "any individual" and stops. A minor can be admitted to the benefits of an unlimited-liability firm but has no defined status in the limited-liability form that is meant to be safer.
2. **India: the director age is not in the statute.** Parliament set 21 for managing directors (s. 196(3)(a)) and nothing for ordinary directors (s. 164). The 18-year floor that every practitioner note repeats could not be traced to a text. The UK (s. 157), Singapore (s. 145(2)) and New York (BCL § 701) put the age in the Act.
3. **India: the law treats the minor as an economic actor in fragments.** A minor may operate a bank account from 10 (RBI), draw a cheque that binds everyone else (NI Act s. 26), act as an agent whose acts bind the principal (Contract Act s. 184), own copyright and a trade mark, and be taxed in his own right on income from his own skill (s. 64(1A) proviso). The one thing he cannot do is make the contract that produces that income.
4. **India: the owner cannot sign and the signer cannot bind the owner.** The minor's own contract is void. The parent cannot act as his agent (s. 183; Raj Rani). The guardian acting as guardian cannot bind him by personal covenant (HMGA s. 8(1)) and cannot bind him to purchases of immovable property (Mir Sarwarjan). What remains is a narrow band of guardian contracts that are within competence and for the minor's benefit (Subrahmanyam).
5. **India: protection that defeats the protected.** Because the agreement is void, not voidable, the minor cannot enforce it either: the actress in Raj Rani and the mortgagee in Mathai Mathai lost. In England, the US and Singapore the minor may enforce and the adult may not escape.
6. **India: shares.** Allotment to a minor is void (Palaniappa, Dalmia), yet the One Person Company rule singles out minors for exclusion, which implies they may be members of other companies. The authority usually given for that (transfer of fully paid shares) was not found.
7. **UK: a director at 16, a contracting party at 18.** A 16-year-old may lawfully run the company but his own undertakings (guarantee, lease, bank mandate) stay voidable for two years. Scotland closes that gap; England and Wales do not.
8. **US: no single rule.** Delaware sets no director age; New York sets 18; and Delaware's custodianship statute treats shareholders as minors until 21. A founder's capacity changes at the state line.
9. **Singapore: capacity at 18, majority at 21, nothing below 18.** The 2009 reform helped 18- to 20-year-olds and left a 16-year-old with no route to register, be a partner, or be a director.
10. **Data-protection law classifies the founder as a child.** Under the DPDP Act every service a 16-year-old founder signs up to must first obtain a parent's verifiable consent, including services he uses to run a business his parents may legally own on paper.

## Reform proposals already made (found)
- Law Commission of India, 13th Report: add an Explanation to Contract Act s. 65 so that restitution applies where a minor misrepresents his age (S). Apparently not enacted.
- Swaminathan and Surana (Statute Law Review): critique of the void rule (abstract read only; their proposal must be read in full).
- England and Wales: Law Com No 134 (1984), enacted as the Minors' Contracts Act 1987 (report N; Act O-IA).
- Singapore: Act 7 of 2009 inserting Civil Law Act ss. 35-36 and the 18-year director rule (O).
- Poland: a draft law on minor entrepreneurs, discussed by Suchański 2023 (article not read in translation).
- No proposal was found, in India or elsewhere among the four, addressed to minors as LLP partners or as directors.

## Corrections to the "Gaps and contradictions" list above (v2)

The list above is copied from the original and is partly out of date. Read it with these changes:

- **Gap 2 (India: director age).** Now: no age in the Act (s. 164, s. 152(3)), but the MCA's own DIR-3 Instruction Kit (2014 and 2023 captures) says "Person should be minimum 18 years of age while applying for this application". So the 18 is traceable, to an instruction on a form, not to a section. "The 18-year floor ... could not be traced to a text" should read "could not be traced to a statute or rule text; it is in an MCA instruction kit".
- **Gap 1 (India: LLP).** Confirmed on the Act, the 2009 Rules and the MCA FAQ: all silent on minors; s. 4 excludes the Partnership Act. One new point: the question was flagged in passing in a 2018 SCC Online (SCC Times) article ("Whether minor can be admitted to the benefits of LLP or whether minor can become a member in LLP as allowed in partnership") and a 2020 iPleaders table states "In LLP minors cannot become partners" - see novelty-final.md.
- **Gap 6 (India: shares).** The authority for the transfer route was found: Padival (CLB 1999), read in full (cases.md D2). Palaniappa and Dalmia (allotment) read in full (D3).
- **Gap 9 (Singapore).** "Nothing below 18" is ACRA's guidance; the statutes read put 18 on the company director (Companies Act s. 145(2)), the LLP manager (LLP Act s. 29(1)) and the business-names authorised representative (BNRA s. 11(2)), and state no age for a sole proprietor, business-names partner or LLP partner (singapore-uk-points.md, 1.2). The source of ACRA's 18 for those roles is unidentified.
- **Age of majority row.** The Indian 21-year limb is gone (1999). Singapore's 21 is supported by Ministry of Law statements (2008-09), not by a located statute.
